What Questions Does This Dashboard Answer?

  • Case Timelines: How long do housing and eviction cases actually take across Maryland—and why can simple averages be misleading?
  • County Differences: Where are cases moving fastest or slowest across Maryland's 24 jurisdictions, and how does local clerk record-keeping affect the numbers?
  • Responsible AI in Courts: Where can courts safely use AI to read legal orders, and where are simple rules or human review more reliable?

Three Headline Findings (In Plain English)

  1. Counting only finished cases hides real wait times. More than half of eviction petitions (53%, or 27,542 cases) were still open at the cutoff date. Looking only at finished cases makes the typical wait look like 46 days—but properly including open cases shows the real midpoint is 65 days (nearly three weeks longer).
  2. Counties vary widely—and local record-keeping matters. Rural counties close warrants much faster (Allegany 12 days, Cecil 15 days) than high-volume urban courts (Anne Arundel 55 days, Baltimore City 54 days, Prince George's 53 days). Just as important, Baltimore City logs almost no Cancelled (61) or Expired (0) warrants in this public feed, so raw county clearance rates cannot be compared fairly without checking local clerk reporting practices.
  3. Combining simple rules with AI beats using AI alone. Tested on 60 real Maryland court orders, simple text rules were instant, free, and just as accurate for predictable items like case numbers and standard rulings. AI (gemini-3.8-flash) proved its value on trickier legal wording—like spotting whether a case was sent back for further court action—boosting accuracy by +24.5 F1 points with 0% made-up quotes.

What This Data Can and Cannot Support

  • Can support: Measuring how much open cases distort court timelines, spotting county-level bottlenecks and record-keeping gaps, and deciding which document fields are safe to automate vs. review by staff.
  • Cannot support: Proving why a court or landlord acted a certain way. Public records do not include tenant legal representation, rent owed, private payment agreements, or Baltimore City's cancelled warrants.

Kaplan–Meier Time-to-Disposition & Survivorship Bias

What this measures: Because 53% of petitions in this window are still open, simply averaging finished cases leaves out the longest-running cases and makes courts look artificially fast (survivorship bias). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis fixes this by including both closed cases and still-open cases up to the September 30 cutoff date to estimate a realistic timeline.

How to read these two charts: Left (Panel A) tracks the share of cases still open as days pass—curves that drop faster mean cases close quicker, with dashed lines at 30, 60, and 90 days. Right (Panel B) compares the misleading closed-case median (gray) against the Kaplan–Meier adjusted median (dark blue); for Public Housing cases, 90% are still open, so the true midpoint has not even been reached yet.

Three Document-Reading Approaches Compared

1. Deterministic Rules (Regex) Hand-written text patterns and keyword lookups (e.g., matching No. 1332). Instant and free—ideal for standardized fields, but misses varied or messy wording.
2. Classical NLP (spaCy + TF-IDF) Traditional lightweight machine learning trained on 20 labeled examples. Strong at spotting dollar entities, but struggles on multi-category legal text without large labeled datasets.
3. Structured LLM (Gemini 3.8 Flash) Modern AI constrained to a strict JSON form, told to answer UNKNOWN when unsure, and required to copy an exact supporting quote from the text so staff can verify every answer.

Pre-Deployment AI Readiness Scorecard (Non-Technical Decision Matrix)

A plain-language deployment guide for court administrators. Green fields can be automated safely with simple rules; Yellow fields benefit from AI assistance with clerk review; Red fields carry high financial/legal risk and should never be auto-posted without human sign-off.

Status Extraction Field Recommended Engine Operational Guidance
GREEN case_number Deterministic Regex 0.950 F1 at 0.15 ms and $0.00. Add multi-docket regex to close the remaining 3% gap without LLM latency.
GREEN disposition_outcome Deterministic Rules + LLM fallback 1.000 F1 on standardized appellate decretal lines. Route unstructured trial-court orders to Yellow review.
YELLOW remand_or_procedural_stay_flag LLM (UNKNOWN + Quote Check) 0.983 F1 (2.3% OCR drop vs. 12.5% for rules). Ideal for human-in-the-loop triage with verbatim quote highlighting.
YELLOW case_category LLM + Human Tie-Break Rules 0.744 F1 (76.7% acc). Real disputes straddle civil/property boundaries; suitable for draft tagging, not binding routing.
YELLOW self_represented_flag Structured Counsel Parser + Clerk Check LLMs can misread solo attorneys without firm names as pro se; cross-check against CMS attorney/bar tables.
RED monetary_judgment_amount Do Not Automate Binding Entry Rules dropped unit multipliers ($147M → $147); LLM abstained on 39% of real amounts. Assistive highlight only.

Privacy Controls Enforced in SQL

  • Downstream scripts query only v_analyst_cases (salted SHA-256 case_id_hash, raw city & 5-digit ZIP removed, coarsened to 3-digit tenant_zip3).
  • Public county/monthly exports enforce strict small-cell suppression (< 10 cases): every published count under 10 is hidden, along with any rate that multiplies back to it. This removes 38 small county-category rows entirely and hides Garrett County's closed and eviction figures.
  • Automated assertions in build_warehouse.py fail the build if any PII column or unsuppressed cell leaks.
